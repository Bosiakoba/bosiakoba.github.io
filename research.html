Research

My research centers on emergency and trauma care in low- and middle-income countries. The areas below reflect work carried out at the Centre of Excellence for Trauma & Emergencies at Aga Khan University, Karachi (Research Fellow and then Research Associate, March 2024 to July 2026).

Traumatic brain injury

I led the analysis for two first-author cohort studies published in JAMA Network Open (August 2026) that quantified 12-month mortality and quality-of-life outcomes among trauma registry patients in Karachi with moderate-to-severe and mild traumatic brain injury. I also first-authored a qualitative study, submitted to The Lancet Regional Health – Eastern Mediterranean, that maps barriers to TBI care using the Four Delay model, drawing on neurosurgeons, emergency physicians and EMS providers to identify candidate interventions. I am currently leading a feasibility study in which a Delphi process narrowed the candidate interventions to three, and I am now assessing their feasibility across four hospitals in Sindh through structured checklists and in-depth interviews. I also composed and submitted an NIH R01 proposal on improving TBI outcomes in low-resource settings, under the mentorship of the principal investigator.

Prehospital emergency care and trauma epidemiology

I clean, manage and analyze national Emergency Medical Services data from Rescue 1122 in Punjab, Pakistan, to answer targeted questions in trauma epidemiology. I first-authored a study on the epidemiological characteristics of elderly patients receiving prehospital care after road traffic injuries in Punjab (International Journal of Emergency Medicine, 2025). Related work with colleagues examines long-term outcomes of traumatic injury in older adults in Pakistan, trauma mortality patterns, and outcomes in emergency general surgery.

Climate and health

I contributed to dissemination of the Elrha-funded HEAT trial, producing a public-facing video that translated the findings into community health messaging and organizing a Heat Consultative Meeting to align district stakeholders on Heat Action Plan (HAP) development. I developed the foundational HAP framework and worked directly with five of Sindh’s most heat-vulnerable districts to build individualized plans; a follow-up consultative meeting presented the completed plans as a feasibility model for the remaining districts and informed provincial (PDMA) documentation. I am leading a qualitative follow-on study of stakeholder-reported barriers to HAP implementation (data collection complete, analysis pending) and a separate study modeling the association between EMS activations and ambient temperature using distributed lag non-linear models (analysis complete, manuscript in preparation).

Disaster preparedness and response

I co-developed a three-tier training program: one-day and four-day trainings for Lady Health Workers and other healthcare workers, and a one-year Disaster Preparedness and Response Leadership Course for managers and sector leaders. I served as Course Coordinator from January 2025 to January 2026, designed and ran simulation drills for course participants, and led a district-level disaster drill that engaged multiple stakeholder roles to stress-test emergency response systems and surface gaps. I am evaluating the effectiveness of the Lady Health Worker and healthcare worker training using the Kirkpatrick Model, covering reaction and knowledge (pre/post tests, with retention re-tested at 6 to 12 months) and behavior change (post-training focus group discussions).

Mentorship and methods

I have mentored Weill Cornell Medicine students during their research visits to Pakistan across project design, ethics (ERC/IRB) submission, data collection, analysis and manuscript writing. I analyze quantitative and qualitative data using R, STATA and NVivo.
